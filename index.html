It started with a map

Before the battle grid and the character sheets, there was a world map — and the handout your players open between sessions.

For the DM: the editor

Drop in any map image and bring it to life: kingdoms and regions, cities and dungeons, roads, rivers and sea routes with names that follow the curve. Every place gets lore in Markdown with [[wikilinks]], NPCs, buildings, a cover image, rumours you reveal one at a time — and DM notes that never leave your screen. Fog what they haven't found.

Already keep your world in Obsidian? ⟳ Sync Vault reads your places, people, factions and hooks straight from your notes — and never writes to your vault.

For the players: the handout

Export Player Map builds a single world-map.html you can drop in the group chat. It opens offline in any browser: parchment styling, zoom and pan, popups with lore, people and places, a compendium, a rumour tracker, a journal, their own notes on every location and a party marker they can move.

Only what you've explored and chosen to share goes in. New session, new export — their notes carry over.

Three tools, one roster

Every tab shares the same characters. Damage on the battle map shows up on the sheet, and the sheet slides in wherever you are.

A battle map that knows the rules

Players click only what they can legally do; the DM can always override.

Turn structure. Action, bonus action, reaction and movement every turn, Extra Attack and Multiattack counted, optional legal-moves enforcement and turn timer.
Spells from the sheet. Slots are spent on the character sheet. Pick targets, assign Magic Missile darts, aim cones, lines and spheres — everyone inside is hit.
Dice that apply themselves. Saves, half damage, resistances, crits, concentration checks, Sneak Attack, Divine Smite and conditions are rolled and applied for you.
Walls, doors and cover. Line of sight, half cover, difficult terrain, and doors and windows with AC and HP you can break.
Fog, light & darkvision. Fog lifts wherever the party can see. Dark dungeons need torches or darkvision, and monsters lurking in the dark stay off the players' screens.
54 SRD monsters. Checked against the SRD, with icons, multiattack, on-hit effects, breath weapons and recharge. Import more from any 5e-database pack.
Prepped encounters. Build maps and monster placements before the session and set them up with a single click.
Six texture packs. Dungeon, wilderness, cave, tavern, classic and high-contrast — or drop in your own background image.

Play online

Your browser stays the game's brain; a tiny standard-library Python server just passes messages. Each player only ever receives what their character could know — never your notes, hidden monsters or what's under the fog.

  1. Run python3 dnd-server.py on your PC or a Raspberry Pi. It prints a DM key and the join address.
  2. In the suite: Encounter → 🌐 Online, connect with the key.
  3. Players open the address, pick a screen name and ask to control their character. You accept — done.

For internet play, forward one TCP port on your router. For online sessions, run your own downloaded copy rather than this hosted one.
